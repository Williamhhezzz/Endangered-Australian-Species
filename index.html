Note: The Y-axis measures relative abundance index change against the historical baseline (1.0 = 100%), not living animal headcounts.

Section 1 of 5 — Divergent Trends

Taxonomic & Functional Group Divergence

Population trajectories vary drastically across animal groups. Monitored amphibians have collapsed to under 5% of baseline levels.

Class-Level Divergence: While terrestrial birds have shown slight resilience, mammal populations have suffered the steepest collapse, plummitting over 60% since 2000. Invasive predator pressures (foxes and feral cats) remain the primary driver of this disparity.

Section 3 of 5 — Legislation

Statutory Protection Categories & Cross-Tabulation

Statutory Status vs. Active Field Monitoring: Commonwealth EPBC Act listings (C4/C5) federally protect 429 vertebrate taxa across Australia. However, TSX active survey coverage (C8/M2) currently tracks 365 species. Federal statutory listing does not automatically guarantee active field ecological monitoring.

Section 4 of 5 — Responses

Risk Escalation & Intervention Outcomes

Land Clearing Impact: Agricultural land clearing destroyed 3.02 million hectares of potential habitat for the Red Goshawk (highest physical area lost), while the Southern Black-throated Finch suffered the worst proportional destruction, losing 10.02% of its entire remaining historical range.

Section 5 of 5 — Pressures

Primary Threat Drivers & Invasive Species

Spotlight Focus — Mammalian Critical Response: Mammals are Australia's primary fauna group with dedicated 3-tier sanctuary tracking data (SoE 2021), serving as a benchmark for invasive predator exclosures.

Sanctuary Intervention Outcomes: Unmanaged mammal populations plummeted by 59 (index = 0.41), whereas populations inside fenced Predator-Free Havens surged by +729% (index = 8.29). Exclosure fence networks represent Australia's most effective immediate recovery tool against invasive predators.
