Since 1985, Australia's overall monitored threatened fauna populations have suffered a steep, continuous collapse. The national index has dropped by 75%, falling from the 1.0 baseline to 0.25.

Critically, even under the upper 95% confidence bound, which is the most optimistic scenario, populations remain severely depressed well below baseline levels, confirming that this decline reflects a systemic ecological crisis rather than localized sampling noise.

Section 1 of 6 — Divergent Trends

Taxonomic & Functional Group Divergence

Population trajectories vary drastically across animal groups. Monitored amphibians have collapsed to under 5% of baseline levels.

Marine mammals show signs of localized recovery, highlighting differences in threat severity and management effectiveness.
