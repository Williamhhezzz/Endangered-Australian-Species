Since 1985, Australia's overall monitored threatened fauna populations have suffered a steep, continuous decline. The national index has fallen by approximately 75% over the past four decades, indicating a persistent crisis across ecosystems.

Section 1 of 6 — Divergent Trends

Taxonomic & Functional Group Divergence

Population trajectories vary drastically across animal groups. Monitored amphibians have collapsed to under 5% of their baseline levels.

Conversely, marine mammals show signs of localized recovery, highlighting how threat severity and management efficacy differ by taxa.

Section 4 of 6 — Responses

Risk Escalation & Intervention Outcomes

Tracking the growth of Critically Endangered species alongside the positive impact of predator-free havens and active management, demonstrating that targeted conservation interventions yield measurable population recoveries.

Section 5 of 6 — Pressures

Primary Threat Drivers & Invasive Species

Invasive predators (such as feral cats and foxes) alongside expansive land clearing remain the leading drivers of fauna extinction risk across the continent.
